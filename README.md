## Project Description

A Business Intelligence analysis of restaurant sales records from a five-city chain (7 Nov 2022 to 29 Dec 2022). The project cleans the raw data, builds executive KPIs, and structures the findings the way a CEO dashboard would: **KPIs -> Trends -> Drivers -> Risks -> Actions**. It also tests how an AI assistant (Gemini) can draft an executive summary when given only verified metrics, and validates its claims against the data.

The dashboard is organised in three pages inside the notebook:

|Page|Content|
|-|-|
|1. Executive Overview|Top 3 KPIs (Total Revenue, Growth %, Average Order Value) and revenue by product|
|2. Sales \& Product Analysis|Weekly trend, weekday pattern, city, product x city and channel analysis|
|3. Customer \& Risk Analysis|Payment behaviour, concentration risks, data-quality scorecard, Risk -> Opportunity -> Action table|

## Dataset

* **Source:** Restaurant Sales dataset from Kaggle:https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rohitgrewal/restaurant-sales-data
* **File in this repo:** `data/restaurant\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\_sales.csv`
* **Size:** 254 sales records, 9 columns, 53 trading days, 5 cities, 5 products, 3 sales channels
* **Columns:** Order ID, Date, Product, Price, Quantity, Purchase Type, Payment Method, Manager, City

## Key Findings

* **Revenue 751,802** with average order value 2,960; weekly revenue grew +30.5% from the first to the last full week (87,200 to 113,800).
* **Burgers generate 50% of revenue** and lead in every city (risk: product concentration).
* **Lisbon and London produce 58% of revenue**; London earns 86% of its revenue online.
* **Online is the largest channel (41%), yet Berlin has no online revenue** and Madrid only 1.6% (opportunity).
* **Data quality:** stray spaces in text columns, 14 spellings for 5 managers, 2 invalid prices (2.3% of raw revenue) and 8 missing Order IDs were found and handled.

## Limitations

* No cost or profit data, so Profit cannot be calculated.
* No customer ID, so customer count, retention and churn cannot be measured.
* Only 53 days of data, including the December holiday season, so growth may be partly seasonal.
* Quantities are decimal, so each row is most likely an aggregated sales record and not a single customer order.
* Two clearly invalid prices were replaced with the product's menu price (documented in the notebook and report).
